PLANNING A SECURITY SYSTEM

Start with the site, risks and daily operations

A useful security system is designed around how a site is actually used. Before selecting equipment, identify entrances and exits, the people who need access, operating hours, lighting conditions, power availability, internet or network coverage, and who will respond when an alert occurs. These details help avoid buying equipment that does not fit the environment.

1. Assess the location

List gates, doors, perimeter areas, reception points, vehicle routes and vulnerable spaces. Note weather exposure, night-time lighting, existing wiring and any constraints that could affect installation or camera views.

2. Match equipment to the need

Choose cameras based on the area to be observed, expected image detail, lighting and storage needs. Gate operators and access controls should suit the gate type, usage frequency, safety requirements and available power. Equipment selection should follow an assessment rather than a one-size-fits-all package.

3. Plan people and response

Decide who can view recordings, manage user permissions, receive alerts and request maintenance. Establish how incidents are reported and how long recordings should be retained, subject to applicable law and the organisation’s privacy obligations.

What NGP can help coordinate

Where specialist equipment or third-party services are required, these should be identified before work begins.

Before requesting a quotation

  • Share the site location and type of property or facility.
  • Describe the main concern: entry control, perimeter visibility, vehicle access, incident review or maintenance of an existing system.
  • Provide an approximate number of gates, entrances or areas to cover, if known.
  • Tell us about power supply, internet connectivity and any existing equipment.
  • Identify the person responsible for approvals and ongoing system operation.

Installation, testing and maintenance

Before accepting a completed installation, users should confirm that the agreed devices work in expected conditions, authorised users can operate the controls, recording and playback functions have been checked where applicable, and instructions for normal use and escalation have been provided. Periodic checks can include camera cleanliness and positioning, storage capacity, date and time settings, access permissions, backup power and gate safety features. Maintenance frequency should reflect the equipment, environment and level of use.

Privacy and responsible use

Surveillance should serve a clear security purpose and be managed responsibly. Inform relevant people where required, restrict access to recordings, protect credentials, avoid unnecessary collection of personal information and establish a suitable retention and deletion process. This page provides general planning information, not legal advice or a substitute for a site-specific risk assessment.
