PROJECT PLANNING

Define the operating need before selecting technology

Infrastructure projects work best when the intended outcome, site conditions and ongoing responsibilities are clear before equipment or implementation methods are chosen. NGP Integrated Services Limited brings engineering, ICT, automation, facility support and project coordination perspectives together to help organisations define requirements and consider workable options.

Requirements and site context

Document what the project must achieve, who will use the system, where it will operate, the existing equipment and services, environmental conditions, available utilities, access constraints and any known safety or regulatory requirements.

Connectivity and monitoring

For connected or remote assets, map where data must travel, how reliable connectivity needs to be, what happens during an outage and who is responsible for reviewing alerts. IoT and satellite communication options should be evaluated against coverage, power, bandwidth, latency, operating cost and maintenance needs.

Delivery and handover

Set out the work packages, responsibilities, dependencies, acceptance checks and documentation required at handover. Agree who will operate and maintain the system after delivery and how faults or changes will be reported.

Areas of project support

Engineering and infrastructure coordination

Early-stage support may include requirements gathering, scope definition, reviewing available specifications, coordinating suppliers or specialist contractors, sequencing activities and recording project assumptions. The final scope depends on the technical complexity, site conditions and the professional services agreed for the project.

IoT and remote asset concepts

Connected monitoring can help teams understand the status of equipment or facilities in locations where regular physical inspection is difficult. A responsible design identifies which measurements are useful, how frequently they are needed, what alert thresholds are appropriate, how communications will be protected and what action a person should take when an alert is received. Monitoring should support a defined operational process rather than collect data without a clear purpose.

Satellite communications planning

Satellite connectivity may be worth assessing where terrestrial network coverage is limited or a resilient communications option is required. Feasibility depends on the service footprint, clear view of the sky, equipment and installation constraints, data requirements, subscription costs, power and local operating conditions. A site-specific review should establish these factors before a solution is selected. This page describes planning considerations and does not claim that a particular satellite service is available at every location.

What to include in an enquiry

  • Project location and a short description of the facility, asset or operation.
  • The problem to solve and the result that would count as success.
  • Existing drawings, specifications, inventory or connectivity details, where available.
  • Known deadlines, budget parameters, access restrictions and safety requirements.
  • The organisation or team that will operate and maintain the completed system.

Quality checks and ongoing support

Before handover, project teams should verify that agreed deliverables match the specification, test relevant functions, record outstanding items, provide operating instructions and clarify maintenance responsibilities. For systems that depend on power or connectivity, checks should include what happens during an interruption and how service is restored. Documentation should be updated when significant changes are made.
